# PLAYTESTS

_The primary validation. Full protocol and templates in `../playtests/`._

## Cadence
- Internal (the two of us): after every meaningful gameplay change.
- Friends: once the greybox loop stands.
- Strangers / Steam Playtest: before committing to production, and again before launch (`../09_STEAM/STEAM_PLAYTEST.md`).

## What we record (per session)
Objectives, setup, observations (facts), player feedback (verbatim), metrics, decisions (KEEP/MODIFY/REMOVE). Templates in `../playtests/_TEMPLATE/`.

## External test protocol (decided 2026-09-16)
16 players, 4 teams of 4, matching the game's own player count. Not more.

| Team | Composition | Why |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Both developers plus 2 others | We watch the game from inside |
| 2 | Friends and acquaintances, left fully autonomous | No guidance, no explanation, we do not play |
| 3 | Strangers | The real signal |
| 4 | Strangers | The real signal |

The two stranger teams are the ones that count. Teams 1 and 2 tell us whether it works, teams 3 and 4 tell us whether it works without us.

Recording and streaming are allowed and encouraged during external tests. The benchmark shows the open beta is a distribution channel as much as a quality tool (`../01_RESEARCH/VIRALITY_PATTERNS.md`, pattern C).

## The greybox gate
One question: is it funny. Spontaneous laughter is mandatory and is the primary criterion. Secondary signals: a player wanting to try something specific, a player wanting to see how the owner reacts, or a player pulled in by the money and the next map. See `../02_GAME_DESIGN/GREYBOX_SPEC.md`.

## The signal we hunt
Spontaneous sequences (a plan, a betrayal, a rescue) that we did not script. That is emergence working.
