# VIRALITY PATTERNS

_How these games actually got seen. Observed in the corpus, Sept. 2026. Distribution is a design problem, not a launch task._

## Thesis
None of the recent hits bought their audience. In every documented case the first wave came from **someone else posting**, because the artefact was cheap to repost and instantly legible. Our job is to make that artefact, not to buy placement.

## A. The reveal asset is the campaign
Dear Passengers reached 700 k wishlists in 36 hours from one trailer that the studio did not distribute. A third-party indie-news aggregator posted it, reached about 47 M views, and the amplification chain ran aggregator, then The Game Awards Instagram, then TikTok, then press. The studio's own account has 435 followers.
**Implication:** the trailer must survive being seen with no context, no sound guarantee, and no brand recognition.

## B. The anti-trailer reads as footage, not as an ad
The Dear Passengers trailer works because the camera is jerky and the voice audio is poor, so it looks like a clip of friends playing rather than marketing. It sells a playground, not a story.
**Implication:** a polished cinematic is worse than a legible bad recording, for this genre.

## C. The pre-launch beta is the seeding
Meccha Chameleon ran a wide international closed beta, around 90% non-Japanese testers, with recording and streaming explicitly allowed. Clips existed before the store page mattered. Zero advertising spend. The game then hit #3 on Twitch with 10.4 M hours watched.
**Implication:** plan the testers as the distribution channel, and remove every barrier to them recording.

## D. Legibility beats production value
The recurring property is that a stranger understands the situation in seconds. This is the same thesis as `WHITE_SPACES.md`, seen from the distribution side rather than the design side.

## E. Concept is not the moat, execution is
Banana Airways launched the same airline co-op concept at the same time as Dear Passengers and collected 11 Steam followers. Same idea, same window, three orders of magnitude apart.

## F. Wishlists are soft until proven
1 M wishlists against about 30 k followers is roughly a 1:35 ratio, where a committed audience sits near 12:1. Industry medians convert 10 to 15% of wishlists into first-week sales, and conversion drops sharply above $10.
**Implication:** never plan revenue off a wishlist count. Plan it off wishlists times 0.10 to 0.15.

## G. Audience overlap is measurable before building
For Dear Passengers, 48% of wishlisters own R.E.P.O., 47% own PEAK, 37% played Meccha Chameleon. The audience for this genre is a known, countable population, not a guess.

## H. Cloning is now near-instant
Meccha Chameleon was cloned within weeks. One Steam copy uses the original's exact name on the Korean store. On Roblox a dozen clones exist, and the two largest together hold more concurrent players than the original does today. Being readable in 10 seconds means being reproducible in 10 days.
**Implication:** the defensible part is whatever is slow to copy, which is feel, tuning and iteration speed, not the premise.

## I. Controversy is not a strategy but it is not fatal
Dear Passengers drew heavy criticism over edgy trailer references and over an AI clause in an old job posting. The founder's assessment was that the coverage brought more attention. Do not plan for it; do not panic if it happens.
