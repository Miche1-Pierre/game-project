The median of the co-op social cluster sits at or below $10. The outliers above $12 are either older, larger in scope, or single-player.

## What the conversion data says
Steam wishlist to first-week sales converts at roughly 10 to 15% on median, and games priced above $10 sit at the low end of that band. For the closest current comparable, analysts recommend $5.99 to $6.99 at launch with a possible increase after the first week, warning that pricing above $10 sharply slows sales in the price-sensitive regions that dominate this audience.

## Team direction (2026-09-16)
**Range accepted: $4.99 to $14.99.** Wider than the corpus band, deliberately.

The strategy is **volume over margin**. The stated goal is a large number of copies at an impulse price rather than a higher price for fewer buyers. A first game sold cheaply to a hundred thousand people is considered a better outcome than the reverse.

Two inputs decide the final number, and neither exists yet:
1. **How the game ends up looking.** If the asset packs and the generated art land well, the upper half of the range becomes defensible.
2. **The Steam wishlist signal.** The wishlist curve before launch is the evidence that settles the price.

A launch promotion is planned regardless of the final number.

## Do not
- Do not set the final price before the greybox and the wishlist curve exist.
- Do not use price as a differentiator. Low price is an entry ticket in this genre, not a position (see `../01_RESEARCH/WHITE_SPACES.md`).
- Do not model revenue from wishlists without applying the 10 to 15% conversion factor.

## Caution on the upper end
The conversion data says games above $10 sit at the low end of the conversion band, and that the price-sensitive regions which dominate this audience drop off sharply above that line. $14.99 and a volume strategy pull in opposite directions. The wishlist curve should settle which one wins, not preference.
