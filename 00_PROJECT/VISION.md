# VISION

_This file defines the kind of product we want to become, before the concept is chosen. It must not name a concept or impose a specific mechanic. The brainstorm decides the game; the vision decides what "good" means for it._

## What We Want to Create
We want to create a small, dense multiplayer game where a simple situation, played with friends, naturally produces funny, tense, chaotic or unexpected stories.

The game should be easy to understand but difficult to fully predict. Players are given a small set of understandable rules, environments and interactions, then allowed to create their own plans and situations.

The most memorable moments should emerge from the interaction between:
- Players.
- The environment.
- Game systems.
- NPCs or other world entities where relevant.
- Time pressure.
- Player mistakes.
- Unpredictable consequences.

The goal is not to tell players a story. The goal is to create the conditions for players to tell their own stories.

## Core Experience
A player should be able to describe a memorable moment in a simple sentence: "We were trying to do X, then Y happened, and everything went wrong."

The best moments should feel immediate, understandable, unexpected, social, replayable, and easy to communicate to someone who was not playing. The game should generate situations that make players naturally react, laugh, panic, blame each other, improvise, or try something completely different on the next attempt.

## North Star
A stranger watching about 10 seconds of gameplay should understand:
1. What the players are trying to accomplish.
2. What is going wrong.
3. Why the situation is interesting.

A normal match should be capable of producing a genuinely shareable moment without the developers having scripted that specific event.

A strong test: can players create a moment that makes a viewer say "what just happened?" while still understanding why it happened?

The game should also remain fun when nobody is recording it. Streamer and creator potential is an important property of the design, but not the sole purpose of the game.

## Why Us
We are a very small team and cannot compete through content volume or AAA production. Our advantage is methodology and iteration speed. We want to:
- Study successful games and understand the principles behind their success.
- Identify recurring patterns without assuming individual mechanics are automatically responsible for success.
- Identify saturated design spaces and opportunities for differentiation.
- Recombine proven principles into something with its own identity.
- Prototype extremely quickly.
- Test ideas through actual gameplay rather than prolonged speculation.
- Use AI-assisted development and asset production to reduce production overhead.
- Invest human time primarily where human judgment creates the most value.

The objective is not to reproduce an existing successful game. It is to understand why successful games work well enough to create something that deserves to exist independently.

## Commercial Ambition
We aim to create a commercially viable indie game capable of competing with successful small-team Steam releases. The ambition is not simply to publish a game. We want a product with:
- A clear reason to play.
- A clear reason to buy.
- Strong replayability.
- Strong social value.
- A distinctive identity.
- Organic word-of-mouth potential.
- Creator and streaming potential.
- Sufficient production quality to be taken seriously as a commercial release.

Commercial success is a design constraint, not something to think about only after development. However, marketability must never replace fun. A game should not be made "viral" through artificial gimmicks if the underlying gameplay does not create genuinely interesting situations.

## Design Philosophy
**Simple rules, complex situations.** The player should understand the basic rules quickly. Complexity should emerge from interactions between simple systems, not from complicated instructions, objectives or interfaces.

**Systems over content.** We prefer systems that produce many situations over large quantities of handcrafted content. One good interaction that creates dozens of situations is more valuable than dozens of scripted situations that happen once.

**Emergence over scripting.** Players create stories through their decisions and mistakes. The team defines the rules of the world rather than manually authoring every memorable moment.

**Fun before production.** A greybox should demonstrate the core fun. Visual quality, assets, animation and polish should amplify a good game, not compensate for a bad one.

**Replayability through variation.** Players replay because situations evolve, strategies change and unexpected interactions occur. Replayability should not depend primarily on grinding, progression or artificially locked content unless those clearly add player value.

**Readability.** The game should be understandable to players and spectators. When something unexpected happens, players should generally understand why. Unexpected does not mean arbitrary.

## What We Do Not Want
- A clone of Lethal Company, R.E.P.O., Among Us or another successful game.
- A game whose primary identity is "the same game, but with a different setting".
- Dependence on large quantities of handcrafted content to stay interesting.
- Extensive backend infrastructure without a clear gameplay requirement.
- Complex progression systems just because commercial games usually have them.
- High-quality assets produced before the gameplay is proven.
- Mechanics added because they are popular elsewhere, without understanding their role.
- Confusing "has proximity voice", "has physics" or "has co-op" with an actual game identity.
- Technical sophistication when a simpler implementation produces the same player experience.
- AI generating large amounts of code or content without validation.
- Visual polish used to hide a weak core loop.
- Scope expanded simply because something is technically possible.
- Complexity for the sake of complexity, or content needed only to fill the game.

## Definition of the Vision
The project succeeds creatively if we can build a game where a small number of understandable rules and interactions consistently produce unpredictable situations that players want to experience, share and replay.

The game should be simple enough to understand quickly, deep enough to generate stories, cheap enough for a very small team to produce, and distinctive enough to deserve its own place on Steam.
